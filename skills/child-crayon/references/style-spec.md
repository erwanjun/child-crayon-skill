# CHILD_CRAYON_V1 — Style Specification

This document defines the fixed visual language. It is a hard boundary, not loose inspiration.

## 1. Priority

When requirements conflict:

1. People count, unique identities, and relationships.
2. Fixed childlike drawing language.
3. Position, pose, and interaction.
4. Clothing and accessories.
5. Background.

“Cute, naive, child-drawn” outranks realistic facial similarity.

Identity should come mainly from hairstyle silhouette, clothing, accessories, body differences, placement, and interaction — not realistic facial anatomy.

## 2. Body proportions

All people, regardless of age or gender, use the same deliberately naive grammar:

- head clearly oversized;
- adult full-body height roughly 2.5–3.2 heads;
- children and babies may be even more top-heavy;
- narrow rounded shoulders;
- short simplified limbs;
- hands like small mittens, with only a few clumsy short finger marks when needed;
- feet/shoes simplified into flattened rounded forms;
- mild asymmetry, skew, and imperfect balance are welcome;
- never use standard adult portrait or fashion-illustration proportions;
- never drift into polished anime chibi or commercial mascot anatomy.

## 3. Face shape

Faces must be broad, round, soft, and slightly irregular:

- round or horizontally oval;
- no pointed chin;
- no defined cheekbones;
- no realistic jawline;
- no mature long face;
- facial marks grouped around the lower-middle of the face;
- generous blank forehead and cheek area;
- every face should feel soft, innocent, calm, and remembered rather than observed anatomically.

## 4. Facial grammar

Keep facial features extremely reduced and consistent across people.

### Eyes

Allowed:

- two tiny black dots;
- two very short curved marks for smiling/closed eyes.

Never:

- eye whites;
- pupils looking in a direction;
- eyelids;
- eyelashes;
- realistic irises;
- detailed eyebrows.

Brows are absent by default.

### Nose

Allowed:

- tiny L-shaped mark;
- tiny angled kink;
- tiny hook curve.

Never:

- nostrils;
- nose wings;
- realistic bridge or shading.

### Mouth

Allowed:

- short straight line;
- short arc;
- tiny simple circle mouth.

Never:

- realistic lips;
- teeth;
- lip color;
- detailed smile anatomy.

### Cheeks

- one obvious pale-pink round crayon blush patch on each cheek;
- soft rough edges;
- visible crayon grain and paper gaps;
- never glossy makeup or airbrushed blush.

### Expression

- gentle, warm, quiet;
- light smile or calm neutral by default;
- no exaggerated anime expression;
- no photorealistic portrait expression;
- slight misalignment and asymmetry are desirable.

## 5. Black line language

The drawing must look made with a blunt, heavily pressed black child’s crayon on white paper.

Required:

- heavy, visually dominant black line;
- uneven thickness;
- slight wobble;
- rough edges;
- local breaks;
- occasional overshoot;
- the same contour often traced by 2–3 imperfect, non-overlapping passes;
- outer silhouette heaviest;
- internal clothing, face, and limb marks somewhat lighter;
- black structure roughly 2–3× the visual thickness of colored fill strokes.

Never:

- one thin accurate continuous contour;
- smooth vector stroke;
- uniform digital outline;
- clean ink illustration;
- precise pen sketch.

## 6. Hair language

Hair is a silhouette plus a small number of heavy messy black crayon strokes:

- broad hairstyle shape first;
- limited but dark curved strokes layered over one another;
- obvious white gaps remain;
- no strand-by-strand rendering;
- no fine pencil hatching;
- preserve the person’s recognizable hairstyle silhouette and color cue without chasing realism.

## 7. Color fill

Use blunt colored crayon / oil-pastel-like loose fill, but preserve the specific child-crayon feel rather than painterly pastel rendering.

Required:

- rough, incomplete fill;
- visible white paper gaps;
- inconsistent stroke direction;
- occasional small overshoot beyond black outline;
- low-to-moderate saturation;
- clothing keeps the photo’s dominant color family while becoming softer and less saturated.

Preferred palette:

- pale pink;
- gray;
- soft blue;
- off-white;
- muted brown;
- deep desaturated blue;
- pale yellow;
- pale green.

Avoid:

- neon color;
- highly saturated pure red over large areas;
- polished solid vector fills;
- smooth gradient;
- realistic highlights;
- reflected light;
- volumetric shading;
- tight cross-hatching.

## 8. Skin handling

Face, ears, neck, and hands should remain mostly the white of the paper.

- do not broadly fill skin with peach/flesh color;
- keep the pale-pink cheek circles;
- at most, use a few very faint warm crayon traces when needed;
- substantial white paper must remain visible.

## 9. Composition

### Single person

- center the subject;
- prefer simple full-body or three-quarter framing;
- preserve hairstyle, clothing, and representative accessory;
- keep generous white space;
- if only a headshot exists, a tiny simple body may be completed conservatively, without inventing elaborate clothing detail.

### Two or more people

- preserve the real number of people and left/right order;
- preserve the core interaction: shoulder touch, leaning, hug, hand-holding, carrying, cuddling, etc.;
- balance visual weight;
- do not make the nearest person absurdly huge;
- gently compress the group into a warm, cohesive cluster;
- avoid formal studio-portrait stiffness.

### Family / parent-child

- keep children naturally central when the original interaction suggests it;
- let parents lean gently toward the child;
- emphasize carrying, hugging, touching, and closeness;
- adults still use round heads, short bodies, and simple dot-feature faces.

## 10. Background

Default: clean bright white paper with large negative space.

Remove or simplify:

- buildings;
- vehicles;
- furniture;
- passersby;
- railings;
- roads;
- clutter;
- realistic lighting;
- deep perspective.

Only preserve sparse symbolic environment cues when they are clearly commemorative, for example:

- a few green scribbles;
- a few simple yellow flowers;
- a small patch of grass;
- one or two simple tree/bush masses;
- a minimal sky/ground hint.

Background marks must be lighter, simpler, and sparser than the people.

Never add borders, captions, signatures, watermarks, or logos.

## 11. Final target

The image should feel like a child used a blunt black crayon plus a few colored crayons on clean white paper to make a sincere keepsake drawing of family or friends:

- thick black messy lines;
- repeated imperfect contours;
- big round heads and tiny bodies;
- dot eyes;
- tiny hook/L nose;
- short-line mouth;
- round pink cheeks;
- loose fill with white gaps;
- simple, clumsy, asymmetrical construction;
- warm, innocent, affectionate mood;
- recognizable relationships and representative identity cues;
- no professional illustration polish.

It must read as a genuine childlike doodle — not a realistic photograph with a crayon filter.

## 12. Hard negatives

Never generate:

- photorealistic portrait;
- realistic adult face shape;
- pointed chin or defined jaw;
- eyebrows/eyelashes/eye whites/realistic eyes;
- realistic nose, nostrils, or lips;
- standard human proportions;
- polished anime chibi;
- American commercial-cartoon style;
- 3D, clay, felt, plush, doll, or toy look;
- watercolor;
- colored-pencil rendering;
- pen sketch;
- clean vector line art;
- uniform digital stroke;
- dense cross-hatching;
- smooth solid fill;
- realistic skin-tone fill;
- gradient, shadow, glossy highlight, or volumetric modeling;
- complex photorealistic background;
- extra person;
- duplicated person;
- merged person;
- extra limb;
- obviously malformed hand detail;
- collage;
- text, logo, signature, watermark, or frame.
